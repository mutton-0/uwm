\subsection{Counterfactual units}
A unit is a logged frame together with an input ego speed. We use the logged speed for every frame,
and for frames from approach sequences also counterfactual speeds of 2--8\,m/s. For each unit we query
the policy three times. The query uses its full input (all cameras, history frames, LiDAR, ego
status), edited in one of three ways: unchanged ($P$), with the target pedestrian removed ($Q$), or
re-rendered at night ($N$). All policies are read on their common horizon of 2.5\,s. Plans and the
pedestrian's \emph{logged} future positions are interpolated to 0.1\,s.

\paragraph{Removal}
The target is located by projecting its annotated 3D box into the camera rather than by taking the
dataset's 2D boxes, whose association to the tracked object is not reliable for the short-range,
partially occluded pedestrians these scenes contain. It is segmented with SAM~\cite{kirillov2023sam}
(box and torso prompts, clipped to the box, dilated by 9\,px) and the mask is filled with
LaMa~\cite{suvorov2022lama}; for the LiDAR policy the points inside the box are deleted as well. The
VLA policies read several cameras and we edit only the front one, so we retain only frames in which
the target appears in no other view.

\paragraph{Night}
A closed-form photometric transform (gamma 2.2, gain 0.45, saturation 0.45, blue tint, sensor noise
$\sigma=7$) is applied to every image the policy consumes. The noise seed is fixed per frame.

\paragraph{Validity}
Every edit is checked by a detector that no policy shares (Faster R-CNN~\cite{ren2015faster}, COCO,
score $\ge0.5$) on the \NumDetN{} front-only frames. It recovers the target in \NumDetOrig\% of the
original images, in \NumDetStill\% after removal and in \NumDetNight\% after the night rendering
(IoU $\ge0.5$). The removal therefore suppresses the pedestrian for an observer outside the policy,
while the night rendering leaves it visible and so changes the lighting rather than the scene. Two
controls bound the residual artefacts: pasting inpainting patches on empty road puts the artefact at
5--17\% of the removal effect at pedestrian scale, and removing \emph{off-road} pedestrians, which
should not matter causally, provides a same-type control.

\subsection{Readouts: the whole trajectory against the pedestrian}
Let $c_t(X)=\|X_t-F_t\|-1.0-0.4$ be the clearance at time $t$ between plan $X$ and the pedestrian's
logged position $F_t$, net of the ego half-width and a pedestrian radius. We read four properties of a
plan:
\begin{itemize}
\item its \emph{separation} $S(X)=\mathrm{mean}_t\min(c_t,10\,\mathrm{m})$, i.e., how far the whole
      trajectory stays from the pedestrian;
\item \emph{contact} $A(X)=\mathbb{1}[\min_t c_t>0]$;
\item \emph{safety distance} $C(X)=\mathrm{clip}(\min_t c_t/1\,\mathrm{m},0,1)$;
\item \emph{time to proximity} $T(X)$, the first time $c_t<1$\,m, divided by 2.5\,s (1 if it never
      happens).
\end{itemize}
Speed changes are deliberately not a readout. The safety of a plan is judged by where it goes relative
to the pedestrian, not by how it decelerates.

\subsection{Exams}
\paragraph{Exposure}
The ratio of planned to logged distance over 2.5\,s, on frames where the human moved at least 0.5\,m.

\paragraph{Hazard sensitivity}
A unit \emph{needs} a reaction if the pedestrian-blind plan comes within 1\,m of the pedestrian,
$C(Q)<1$. On such units,
\begin{equation}
\HS=\tfrac14\big[\Delta A+\Delta C+\Delta T+\tanh(\Delta S/1\,\mathrm{m})\big]\in[-1,1],
\end{equation}
with $\Delta=(\cdot)(P)-(\cdot)(Q)$: the safety that seeing the pedestrian buys. We plot $\HS$
against the model-independent hazard level $a_{\mathrm{req}}=v^2/2\max(d-2,0.5)$, the deceleration
needed to stop before a pedestrian at distance $d$, and alternatively against $\mathrm{TTC}_0=(d-2)/v$.
Units with input speed below 1\,m/s are excluded, because a stationary ego cannot move toward or away
from anyone.

\paragraph{Scaling and specificity}
\emph{Scaling} is the rank correlation of $\HS$ with $a_{\mathrm{req}}$ across needed units.
\emph{Specificity} applies the minimal-change principle to the units that need no reaction:
$\SP=\exp(-\bar D(P,Q)/0.5\,\mathrm{m})$, where $\bar D$ is the mean displacement between the two
plans.

\paragraph{Lighting}
For the same frames, $\CFR=\mathbb{E}\|P-Q\|/\mathbb{E}\|P-N\|$ compares the plan change caused by
removing the pedestrian with that caused by the night rendering. Alignment is the median cosine between
$N-P$ and $Q-P$. A pure policy has $\CFR\gg1$ and alignment near zero. Positive alignment means the
night pushes the plan the way removing the pedestrian does.

\subsection{Policies and frames}
We examine LTF and DiffusionDrive (front camera and ego status), DiffusionDriveV2 (camera and LiDAR),
SimLingo (InternVL2-1B), AutoVLA (Qwen2.5-VL-3B; three cameras $\times$ four frames) and Alpamayo-1.5
(four cameras; a diffusion head over the VLM cache). All use released weights. The frames are
nuScenes~\cite{caesar2020nuscenes} keyframes. They cover corridor pedestrians (lateral clearance to the
ego corridor $\le1$\,m) at 5--12\,m, their off-road counterparts, and 30 approach sequences at
5.8--14.6\,m with logged pedestrian futures. The near-pedestrian units come from 236 front-only
frames, 88 in Boston (left-hand drive) and 148 in Singapore (right-hand drive). Alpamayo-1.5 covers
170 of the 236, since it needs 1.6\,s of history and 6.4\,s of future within the scene. We fix
DiffusionDrive's diffusion seed so that paired queries share their noise.
