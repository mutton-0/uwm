\section{Lighting: Magnitude, Direction, and the Pedestrian}
\label{sec:lighting}

\input{tables/tab_light}

Lighting should not change what a driver does, so this exam asks how far a policy is moved by an
irrelevant change. It is the most robust result in the check-up and the one whose direction is least
stable.

\paragraph{Magnitude}
Removing a pedestrian from the corridor moves every policy's plan only \NumCFRlo--\NumCFRhi{} times as
far as the night rendering does, with every 95\% interval below one (\cref{tab:light}); on
detector-verified edits $\CFR=0.30$--$0.64$, and only Alpamayo-1.5's interval (45 frames) reaches
above one. On a separate pool of 312 lead-vehicle events the effect is carried by sensor noise rather
than by darkness: for DiffusionDrive, darkening alone raises the planned speed by $+0.08$\,m/s and
darkening with noise by $+2.05$\,m/s. It is also global---perturbing only the sky recovers 19\% of the
effect of deleting the lead vehicle.

\paragraph{Direction: faster, and therefore closer}
A policy that slows at night is doing something defensible; one that speeds up is not. Three policies
plan to go faster under the night rendering: DiffusionDrive by \NumNightDD\%, SimLingo by
\NumNightSL\% and LTF by \NumNightLTF\%, and their whole plans pass $0.24$--$0.47$\,m closer to the
pedestrian. The approach is longitudinal: the further the plan's endpoint moves forward, the closer it
gets (rank correlation $-0.73$ to $-0.96$), while the endpoint shifts toward the pedestrian's side no
more often than chance. DiffusionDriveV2, the only policy that also reads LiDAR, slows
($\NumNightDDv\%$) and keeps more distance; AutoVLA and Alpamayo-1.5 are unaffected.

\paragraph{The two directions in representation space}
We write $\hat v_F$ for the \emph{faithfulness} direction ($Q{-}P$, the response to the pedestrian) and
$\hat v_I$ for the \emph{interference} direction ($N{-}P$, the response to a change carrying no
driving-relevant information); these are unit vectors in representation space, the activation-side
counterparts of the behavioural $F$ and $I$ of \cref{sec:axes}. The two are not merely uncorrelated:
in aggregate they oppose one another (mean cosine $-0.54$ over four policies), so a policy encodes
``it got dark'' as it encodes ``the hazard is gone''. We had expected policies with more orthogonal
directions to shift less between domains; they shifted \emph{more} (\cref{tab:prereg}, P6), and
alignment itself changes with the driving side for all but AutoVLA. The magnitude of the lighting
effect is a trait a small check-up can measure; its direction is not, until hundreds of frames are
available (\cref{sec:sample}).
