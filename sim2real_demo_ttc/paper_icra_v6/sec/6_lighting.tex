\section{Lighting: Magnitude, Direction, and the Pedestrian}
\label{sec:lighting}

\input{tables/tab_light}

Lighting should not change what a driver does, so this exam asks how far a policy is moved by an
irrelevant change. It is the most robust result in the check-up, the verdict that transfers between driving sides for every policy (\cref{sec:sample}), and the one whose direction is least stable.

\paragraph{Magnitude}
Removing a pedestrian from the corridor moves every policy's plan only \NumCFRlo--\NumCFRhi{} times as
far as the night-style perturbation does, with every 95\% interval below one (\cref{tab:light}); on
detector-verified edits $\CFR=0.30$--$0.64$, and only Alpamayo-1.5's interval (45 frames) reaches
above one.

\paragraph{Two lighting levels}
The perturbation is applied at two strengths, $\mathcal{E}_D$ (dusk) and $\mathcal{E}_N$ (night), the milder dusk level chosen to keep the pedestrian visible. Of the \NumDuskDetN{} targets the detector finds in the original, it still finds
\NumDuskDetDusk\% at dusk and \NumDuskDetNight\% at the night level (removal: \NumDuskDetRm\%). On
the same \NumDuskN{} scenes the ratio is $\NumDuskLo$--$\NumDuskHi$ at dusk and
$\NumDuskNightLo$--$\NumDuskNightHi$ at the night level: below one for every policy at both levels,
with the pedestrian in plain view.

\paragraph{Direction: faster, and therefore closer}
A policy that slows under the perturbation is doing something defensible; one that speeds up is not. Three policies
plan to go faster under the night-style perturbation: DiffusionDrive by \NumNightDD\%, SimLingo by
\NumNightSL\% and LTF by \NumNightLTF\%, and their whole plans pass $0.24$--$0.47$\,m closer to the
pedestrian. The approach is longitudinal: the further the plan's endpoint moves forward, the closer it
gets (rank correlation $-0.73$ to $-0.96$), while the endpoint shifts toward the pedestrian's side no
more often than chance. DiffusionDriveV2, the only policy that also reads LiDAR, slows
($\NumNightDDv\%$) and keeps more distance; AutoVLA and Alpamayo-1.5 are unaffected.

\paragraph{Direction is not a stable trait}
The two are not merely uncorrelated: in aggregate they oppose one another (mean cosine $-0.54$ over
four policies), so, on the pooled data, a policy encodes ``it got dark'' in a direction close to the
one for ``the hazard is gone''. We report this as an observation rather than as evidence for a stable
property: the opposition does not hold per policy, alignment changes with the driving side for all but
AutoVLA (\cref{tab:light}), and the prediction built on it (P6, \cref{tab:prereg}) went the other way.
