\section*{Appendix}

\subsection*{A. Measurement decisions that changed the numbers}

We record four decisions whose alternatives we tried and rejected, because each moved a
headline number by more than the effect it was measuring.

\paragraph{A.1 Fill rule for object removal.}
Three fills on 304 deployment lead events: a grey blob (the frame's mean colour), the median
colour of the road surface immediately below the box, and that colour plus Gaussian noise
matched to the local texture.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Fill & DD & LTF & DDv2 \\
\midrule
grey blob            & $+0.214$ & $-0.152$ & $+0.244$ \\
road colour $+$ noise & $+0.880$ & $+1.309$ & $+0.287$ \\
road colour only     & $+0.234$ & $-0.153$ & $+0.240$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The noiseless road colour reproduces the grey blob almost digit for digit; the noise inflates
$b$ four- to eight-fold and moves the control arm from $-0.008$ to $+0.204$. All results use
the noiseless road fill. DiffusionDriveV2, the only LiDAR consumer, is the least affected,
which is consistent with its response being dominated by the point cloud.

\paragraph{A.2 Chord versus arc.}
Planned speed is read as full arc length over the horizon rather than the chord to the final
waypoint. A policy that swerves around a hazard rather than braking would otherwise score as
not responding. On identical events the ground-truth magnitude reads $0.332$ under a
chord-based $0.25$--$0.75$\,s window and $1.232$ over the full horizon.

\paragraph{A.3 Layer selection is not one criterion but three.}
Selecting layers by \emph{reading} quality, by \emph{predictive} value, and by \emph{causal}
reach gives nearly disjoint answers. For DiffusionDrive the reading-quality gate selects $L_5$;
$L_5$'s causal reach ($|\Delta y|/\alpha$ under random-direction injection) is $1/10$ of $L_6$'s
and $1/34$ of $L_0$'s; and $L_7$'s reach is exactly zero---the trajectory head does not consume
it---although it passes the reading gate. Weighting layers by held-out agreement with the target
raises the projection--response correlation from $-0.036$ to $+0.154$ (DD) and $-0.111$ to
$+0.179$ (LTF) under 5-fold cross-validation, against a label-shuffled null of $+0.076$ and
$-0.028$. Behavioural information is present but not in the layer the reading criterion picks.

\paragraph{A.4 Multi-camera policies need every view masked.}
The two vision--language policies consume 3--4 cameras $\times$ 4 frames. Masking only the
front camera's current frame covers $1/12$ to $1/16$ of the visual input; on 49 events the
object remained visible in the front camera's three history frames in 47 cases, and
Alpamayo-R1 has \emph{two} front slots (120$^\circ$ and 30$^\circ$) of which the earlier
implementation masked one. Correcting this moves AutoVLA's $b$ from $-0.007$ (indistinguishable
from zero) to $+0.123$.

\subsection*{B. Why the deployment side is small}

Four independent criteria, applied to the same right-hand-drive data:

\begin{center}\footnotesize
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule
Criterion & yield & of which dangerous \\
\midrule
brake-first mining              & 74 ev. & 0 (min.\ TTC $2.3$\,s) \\
TTC screening, gated            & 330 sc. & 0 below $1.5$\,s \\
harsh braking $\ge 3$\,m/s$^2$  & 31 ev. & 11 with object ahead \\
NAVSIM RHD split                & 1{,}354 sc. & 0 above $2$\,m/s$^2$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The TTC row needs three gates to be usable: excluding roadside furniture, requiring the object
to be genuinely ahead ($d_{\mathrm{long}} \ge 5$\,m, $|{\rm lat}| \le 1.2$\,m), and excluding
cross-traffic (object longitudinal velocity $\ge -2$\,m/s). Without them the screen returns 55
``dangerous'' scenes, all of which are barriers and cones passed at $0.1$--$0.6$\,m or vehicles
crossing an intersection; we verified this by inspecting frames.

\subsection*{C. Per-city hours}

nuPlan publishes no per-city breakdown. The final two integers of a log's filename are its
start and end second offsets, so their difference is the log's exact duration; we verified this
against \texttt{lidar\_pc} timestamp spans on four databases (agreement to the second). Reading
only the zip central directories:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
City & logs & hours & with sensors \\
\midrule
Las Vegas        & 9{,}077 & $770.5$ & $76.8$ (10.0\%) \\
Singapore (RHD)  & 2{,}909 & $177.3$ & $\phantom{0}6.9$ (\phantom{0}3.9\%) \\
Pittsburgh       & 1{,}910 & $129.8$ & $11.4$ (\phantom{0}8.8\%) \\
Boston           & 2{,}014 & $\phantom{0}96.7$ & $15.8$ (16.4\%) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Train-split log counts sum to $13{,}180$, matching the published figure. Our total of
$1{,}174$ hours is $22\%$ below the $1{,}500$ stated in the dataset paper; per-log durations are
exact, so the gap is either rounding or material not publicly released, and we could not
determine which.
